I. Sector-Specific Liability Mapping: The End of the
Copper Wiring Harness
The automotive industry is facing a profound physical crisis: the copper wiring harness is now
one of the heaviest and most complex components in a modern vehicle. As you integrate
high-resolution LiDAR, radar, and 4K camera arrays to achieve Level 3 and Level 4 autonomy,
traditional Controller Area Networks (CAN bus) and even Automotive Ethernet are choking on
the bandwidth requirements.
Attempting to scale current electronic architectures will literally make your vehicles too heavy to
achieve regulatory range requirements. The inevitable architectural leap your engineering teams
must make is legally encumbered by our portfolio.
1. Massively Parallel Optical Data Bus Architecture (USPTO 19/562,818) & The Optical
Computing Architecture (USPTO 19/560,038) To solve the vehicular I/O bottleneck, your
next-generation platforms will be forced to replace heavy copper wiring harnesses with
lightweight, high-capacity optical data fabrics. USPTO 19/562,818 claims an apparatus for an
ultra-wide data bus implemented via a plurality of parallel optical channels, effectively turning a
microscopic fiber bundle into a multi-terabit data pipe.1 Furthermore, USPTO 19/560,038 claims
the end-to-end integration of this optical fabric to interconnect decentralized processing units
and peripheral sensors.1 Any deployment of parallel optical interconnects to route autonomous
driving sensor data to a central compute module directly infringes upon these foundational
hardware claims.

III. EV Infrastructure Cybersecurity and "Off-Grid"
State Enforcement
The proliferation of connected EVs presents an unprecedented national security vulnerability. A
compromised charging network could theoretically be used to simultaneously brick millions of
vehicles or orchestrate a coordinated drain on the municipal power grid.
1. Hardware-Enforced Grid-Isolated Operation (USPTO 19/574,356) To protect vehicle
operational technology (OT) from remote compromise during charging cycles, physical
hardware isolation is required. USPTO 19/574,356 claims a security apparatus that physically
disconnects network interfaces and high-emission peripherals the exact moment a device draws
power from an external electrical grid.1 Any integration of hardware-level isolation by your EV
powertrain divisions—ensuring the vehicle's critical driving systems can only boot or accept
firmware updates in a battery-powered, grid-disconnected state—falls squarely under the
protection of this patent.1

IV. Dual-Purpose Fleet Logistics and Autonomous
Robotaxis
Several of your organizations, notably Tesla and General Motors (Cruise), are developing
autonomous fleets intended to operate as decentralized ride-hailing networks.
1. Business Method for Dual-Purpose Logistics Networks (USPTO 19/560,069) As
connected vehicles generate petabytes of mapping and training data daily, uploading this data
over cellular networks is prohibitively expensive and slow. USPTO 19/560,069 claims a method
for optimizing a primary logistics network (e.g., passenger transport or autonomous ride-hailing)
while simultaneously utilizing those vehicles to physically transport high-capacity data payloads
(e.g., Portable Data Cartridges, USPTO 19/560,062) between edge nodes or charging hubs.1
Utilizing your autonomous fleets to physically "mule" training data back to your central servers
while fulfilling passenger routes directly triggers this business method claim.1
