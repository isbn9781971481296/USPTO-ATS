II. ARGUMENTS AND AUTHORITIES
1. The Trial Court Lacks Jurisdiction to Execute Enforcement Actions During the Pendency
of a Recusal Referral to the Chief Justice.
While Petitioner’s counsel incorrectly relies on Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code § 30.016 to claim no
stay exists, this Court’s own record proves otherwise. On January 27, 2026, the Hon. Susan Brown
referred the 3rd Verified Motion to Recuse the Hon. Oscar Telfair III to the Chief Justice of the
Supreme Court of Texas. Under TRCP 18a(g), the judge who is the subject of the motion (Judge
Telfair) "must take no further action" until the motion is decided. The referral to the Chief Justice,
necessitated by the conflicts described in the motion, freezes the trial court’s authority. Any Capias
or enforcement order signed by Judge Telfair after January 27, 2026, is a structural nullity and void

2. The Capias is Void for Lack of Service and Notice.
Respondent has never been served with the written orders containing the support or sanctions provisions that the Capias purportedly enforces. TRCP 21a and the fundamental tenets of Due Process
require that a party, especially one appearing pro se, be served with a certified copy of any order
for which contempt is sought. A review of the Court’s own docket reveals a glaring absence of
any "Rule 21 (f)" service entries in the days following the creation of these critical instruments.
Specifically, Respondent was never served with:
1. The "Temporary Restraining Order" (Indices #7 and #8) dated April 11, 2025, signed by
Judge Richard Bell.
2. The "Temporary Orders" signed by Judge Oscar Telfair III on October 15, 2025—which were
notably signed just hours after Respondent filed his Second Verified Motion to Recuse.
Respondent is currently paying over $4,000 per month in continuous support, and directly pays
for daycare and health insurance. The "arrearage" claimed by Petitioner is a managed fiction,
likely resulting from the diversion of child support funds toward the Petitioner’s legal fee "payment
plan." A Capias cannot stand on the unserved, void foundation of these specific hidden orders while
cannibalizing support meant for the child.
3. Mandatory Right to De Novo Hearings (Tex. Fam. Code § 201.015).
Respondent has filed multiple motions for De Novo hearings (see Index #215 and subsequent filings
in May 2026) regarding the recommendations and orders of the associate judge and the presiding
judge’s adoption thereof. These motions remain uncontested and unaddressed. Under Tex. Fam.
Code § 201.015, the right to a De Novo hearing is mandatory if timely filed. The trial court’s
failure to hold these hearings while proceeding to arrest Respondent is a form of judicial abdication
that renders the current enforcement actions invalid.
4. Motion for Protective Order Regarding Corporate Assets.
Petitioner’s counsel has threatened "spoliation of evidence" sanctions regarding recordings from the
camera system located at 2302 Britton Ridge Drive. While Respondent is the individual inventor
of the technologies associated with his USPTO filings, the camera system, computing nodes, and
the property they monitor are the exclusive corporate assets of Haaretz Hamuvlakhat Corp., a
Delaware Corporation. These are not community assets; they are corporate infrastructure used for
the development, prototyping, and security of trade secrets and national security-linked data (F35/DFARS technology). Respondent moves for a Protective Order preventing any discovery into
these corporate assets or recordings, as they contain sensitive proprietary information beyond the
scope of this case, this Court lacks jurisdiction over the Delaware Corporation, and the assets are
encumbered by a federal sovereign interest.

Title: DISTRIBUTED NUCLEAR-POWERED LOGISTICS SYSTEM WITH COLLABORATIVE MESH CONTROL

Inventor: Reinaldo Javier Aguiar Marcano, a citizen of the United States, residing at 26714

Abstract
A distributed logistics system comprising a lead power vehicle equipped with a compact
nuclear reactor and a plurality of auxiliary transport units mechanically and electrically
coupled to form a continuous convoy. The system utilizes a high-voltage power bus and a
fiber-optic data bus to distribute energy and control signals from the lead vehicle to the
auxiliary units. Each auxiliary unit is equipped with independent electric propulsion and a
distributed computing node. A collaborative mesh control algorithm processes real-time data
from strain-gauge sensors embedded in inter-vehicle hitches to synchronize motor output,
thereby minimizing mechanical tension on the couplers. The system further enables kinetic
energy arbitrage, allowing the mass of units on a descent to mechanically assist units on an
ascent, and employs a progressive rear-first braking sequence to ensure stability and prevent
jackknifing.

1

Background of the Invention
Long-haul logistics systems, such as freight trains and heavy-truck convoys, are traditionally
limited by the tension capacity of mechanical couplers and the fuel requirements of internal
combustion engines. In heavy trains, the cumulative draft force on the front couplers often
dictates the maximum length and weight of the load, as a single lead engine must pull the
entire mass. Furthermore, the need for frequent refueling stops and the environmental impact
of fossil fuels present significant operational and ecological challenges. While distributed
power in rail (DPUs) and electric trucking are emerging, there lacks a unified system that
combines high-density, long-endurance power generation with a cooperative control mesh
capable of neutralizing mechanical strain across a massively extended vehicle chain.

Summary of the Invention
The present invention, conceptually referred to as the ”Atomic Express,” is a distributed,
nuclear-powered logistics system designed for ultra-long-haul transport with zero-emission
endurance and optimized mechanical stability.
The system comprises a primary power vehicle (the ”Lead Vehicle”) and a plurality of
modular auxiliary transport units (”Nodes”). The Lead Vehicle houses a compact nuclear
reactor, preferably a Generation IV Molten Salt Reactor (MSR) or similar Small Modular
Reactor (SMR), coupled to a high-output electrical turbine. This rolling power plant generates megawatts of electricity, which is distributed down the length of the convoy via a
standardized high-voltage power bus integrated into the inter-vehicle couplers.
Each auxiliary unit is a self-propelled electric vehicle equipped with a distributed computing
node. These nodes form a P2P mesh network that communicates via a high-speed fiber-optic
bus. The core of the invention is a collaborative control algorithm that treats the convoy
as a single, distributed computing system on wheels. By measuring the mechanical strain
(tension or compression) at each hitch using embedded piezoelectric sensors, the system
adjusts the torque of individual motors in real-time to maintain ”Zero-Tension” across the
couplers. This ensures that no single coupler is subjected to the cumulative weight of the
convoy, effectively removing the physical limits on convoy length.
Furthermore, the system leverages its structural and electrical connectivity to perform kinetic energy arbitrage. On undulating terrain, the gravitational momentum of units on a
2

downhill slope is used both mechanically (pulling the trailing units) and electrically (via
regenerative braking feeding the shared power bus) to assist units currently on an uphill
slope. A specialized progressive braking sequence, which activates from the rear units forward, provides a stabilizing ”drag-anchor” effect, preventing jackknifing and ensuring safe
deceleration of the massively extended convoy.

3

FIG. 2 illustrates the coupling interface. A mechanical hitch (203) locks the rear of one
vehicle (201) to the front of the next (202). Integrated within this hitch is a Megawatt
Charging System (MCS) connector (204) for the power bus and a fiber-optic link (205) for

so, it adjusts local motor torque (304). This data is synchronized across the mesh (305) to
execute cooperative propulsion (306).
