I. Statement of Jurisdiction and Mandatory Nature of
this Motion
This Court is presently under a mandatory, automatic stay of all proceedings pursuant to
Texas Rule of Civil Procedure 18a(f)(1)(A) and Texas Government Code § 22.004(c). This
stay arises from Relator’s Third Verified Motion to Recuse (Index #149), filed January 14,
2026, which was referred by the Hon. Susan Brown to the Chief Justice of the Supreme
Court of Texas on January 27, 2026 (Index #170). The stay remains in full force and effect.
Relator appears now only in a limited capacity to file this Motion, which the Court has a
non-discretionary, mandatory duty to address under Texas Family Code § 201.015. This
duty persists irrespective of the appellate stay, as it concerns the very foundational integrity
of this case and seeks to prevent ongoing and future harm arising from an unserved, secret
order from over a year ago. The imminent threat of arrest via the proposed Capias—which
this Motion will demonstrate is predicated on the void 2025 proceedings—transforms this
from a stale issue into a live, urgent controversy requiring immediate judicial attention. To
ignore this motion under the guise of the stay would be a constitutional violation; to act
upon it would require the Court to acknowledge its mandatory duties.

II. Argument and Authorities: The Jurisdictional Trap
A. The Secret Ex Parte Hearing of April 2025 and The Unserved
Order
The official docket of this case, attached as Exhibit A, reveals a profoundly disturbing
sequence of events in April 2025:
• April 9, 2025: Opposing Counsel files a "Proposed Order" deceptively titled "Temporary Restraining Order and Order Setting Hearing for Temporary Orders" (Index
#5).
• April 11, 2025: A "Judge Docket Entry" states: "TRO Signed - RB" (The Hon.
Richard Bell). This resulted in the issuance of a "Temporary Restraining Order" (Index
#8).
• April 11, 2025: The Clerk issues a "Citation" (Index #9) corresponding to this TRO,
which is officially marked on the docket as "Unserved."
Relator was never notified of, summoned to, or present at any hearing that resulted in
this TRO. Five days later, on April 16, 2025, Relator was served not with the actual TRO
(Index #8), but with the deceptively titled "Notice of Hearing" (Index #5), attached hereto
as Exhibit B. A plain reading of Exhibit B shows it contains no operative restraining
orders; it is merely a notice for a future hearing on May 15, 2025.
This sequence constitutes a textbook violation of due process and potentially implicates
state and federal criminal statutes concerning the deprivation of rights under color of law
(e.g., 18 U.S.C. § 242). An order was entered against Relator in secret, and its existence
was concealed from him. The provisions of that TRO may still be operative today, and the
subsequent Capias is directly tied to the enforcement of orders stemming from this tainted
procedural foundation.

B. The Right to a De Novo Hearing is Absolute and its Timeline
Was Never Triggered
Texas Family Code § 201.015(a) mandates that a party is entitled to a de novo hearing
before the referring court. While subsection (b) requires a request for such a hearing to be
filed not later than the third working day after the date the associate judge’s proposed order
is received, this timeline is predicated on the constitutional minimum of notice.
Here, the clock never started. Relator was never served with the associate judge’s (or, in
this case, the predecessor judge’s) TRO. One cannot be expected to appeal an order whose
existence is unknown. Therefore, Relator’s right to a de novo hearing on the secret TRO of
April 11, 2025, is preserved and is now ripe for adjudication, especially as the "fruit of the
poisoned tree"—the Capias—threatens his liberty.

III. PRAYER
For these reasons, Relator Reinaldo J. Aguiar Marcano respectfully prays that this Court,
in its mandatory duty, enter an Order:
1. GRANTING an immediate de novo hearing on the issues and evidence related to
the Temporary Restraining Order (Index #8) signed on April 11, 2025.
2. CLARIFYING this Court’s official position on whether it is bound by the mandatory,
automatic stay pending the Supreme Court of Texas’s ruling on the motion to recuse.
3. COMPELLING the production of all evidence, testimony, or exhibits presented during the secret hearing of April 2025 that was used to justify the TRO.
4. COMPELLING the immediate production of a certified copy of the complete, signed,
and unserved Temporary Restraining Order (Index #8).
