I. The Jurisprudential Paradox: Eminent Domain vs.
The Perpetual License
Under standard operating procedures defined by 28 U.S.C. § 1498, the United States
Government exercises eminent domain over domestic patents, shielding its defense
contractors from civil injunctions and assuming financial liability for infringement.1
However, the situation at hand introduces a critical jurisprudential paradox. The inventor of the
Aguiar Marcano Intellectual Property Portfolio—which comprises 52 foundational patent
applications published defensively in The Architecture of Control (ISBN
978-1-97-148129-6)—has proactively granted an irrevocable, perpetual, no-fee license to the
federal government for all national defense purposes.1 Consequently, the U.S. Government
owes absolutely no royalties for the utilization of these systems.1
The legal friction immediately shifts. Defense contractors (Palantir and Anduril) are currently
utilizing these specific patented architectures and subsequently embedding the research,
development, and licensing costs of these technologies into their multi-billion-dollar
government contracts.1 They are effectively charging the American taxpayer for intellectual
property that the government already possesses the legal right to utilize indefinitely and
without cost.1
This scenario transcends standard patent infringement; it borders on procurement fraud,
waste, and abuse of federal funds.1 Failing to halt the disbursement of funds to these
contractors for pre-licensed intellectual property constitutes complicity in corporate
intellectual property theft and a gross mismanagement of taxpayer dollars.1
