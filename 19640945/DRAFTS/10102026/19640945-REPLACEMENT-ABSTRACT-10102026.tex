\begin{center}
    \textbf{\large ABSTRACT OF THE DISCLOSURE}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.6cm}

A method for secure communication that inverts the traditional cryptographic paradigm to significantly increase resilience against man-in-the-middle attacks. The method comprises transmitting an encrypted message, $M$, encrypted with a first key, $K_1$, that is never transmitted. Subsequently, a prefix, $P$, and a suffix, $S$, are transmitted independently. A second key, $K_2$, is then derived from $K_1$ and the full concatenated data block ($P+M+S$). Only after these transmissions are complete is $K_2$ transmitted to the recipient. An attacker must successfully intercept four separate and asynchronous transmissions, potentially across different channels, and correctly associate them to compromise the communication.
